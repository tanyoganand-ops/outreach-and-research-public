## 4. What tonight's numbers actually show (observations, not market statistics)

Observed on one board on one night - real but narrow evidence:

1. **Generic design briefs in this sample carried higher visible proposal counts.** Logo, website, flyer and template briefs showed 30-81 proposals at budgets of $78-$200; the two pitch-deck briefs showed 36-45 at £88-£120. A proposal count is visible interest from bidders on one board - it does not measure total designer supply, and no general supply claim is made.
2. **Specialist technical design briefs drew noticeably fewer proposals.** 3D, CAD and structural briefs showed 3-13 proposals versus 30-81 on generic briefs at comparable budgets ($40-$221). Read with care: a lower count can mean a newer brief, a low budget, or niche tooling rather than a shortage of able designers, and a proposal count shows visible interest on one board, not total supply. Treated here as a hint worth testing, not as evidence of undersupply.
3. **UGC buyers on the open UK board are small businesses with small budgets** (£10-£250 typical live, up to £1,000 for a regulated corporate campaign), and two of five explicitly flex scope to budget. The larger per-creator money observed tonight (£100-£1,000) came with compliance constraints (regulated weight-management category, scripts, eligibility criteria).
4. **Usage rights are a live negotiation point in real briefs.** Two live briefs ask the creator to state usage duration, scope and licensing fees; one separates organic from paid-ad usage. This is brief-level evidence that rights pricing is expected - not a rate range, and no rate range is claimed.

**Positioning hypotheses for you to test (each is a hypothesis, none is established):**
- H1: Technical/specialist design (3D, CAD, motion, interactive) may face less per-brief competition than generalist design on open boards; tonight's lower proposal counts are consistent with that but also with newer briefs, low budgets or niche tooling. Test: watch PPH/Twine specialist categories for 2-4 weeks, tracking brief age and budget alongside proposal counts.
- H2: A creator who prices usage rights explicitly looks more professional to SMB buyers than one who does not, because buyers are already asking for it. Test: include a rights line in any future quote and track responses.
- H3: Regulated/compliance-sensitive UGC (health, finance) may pay more per creator but demand comfort with scripts and eligibility rules. Limitation: the higher figure (£100-£1,000) comes from one closed THG/ASDA campaign - a single expired sample cannot establish a category premium. Test: monitor ugccreators.co.uk and Fluencify for the next regulated-category campaign and compare like-for-like deliverables.
