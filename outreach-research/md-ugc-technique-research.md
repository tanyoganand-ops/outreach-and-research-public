# 1. Rules you must follow (verified requirements)

## Ad and sponsored-content disclosure

If a brand pays you in money, gifts or discounts, the post is an ad. The label rules differ by country, so follow the stricter one when unsure.

- **[Official, US FTC]** Disclose any financial, personal or family relationship, including free products. Put the disclosure in the video itself, not only in the description. Audio plus on-screen text is best, since some people watch muted. Do not hide it in hashtags or behind "more". Use plain words: "ad", "advertisement", "sponsored", or "thanks to X for the free product". Avoid "sp", "collab", "ambassador" alone. Do not claim experience with a product you have not tried. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers
- **[Official, UK ASA/CAP]** Expect to need a prominent "Ad" label upfront, visible without tapping anything. "Sponsored", "gifted", "thanks to", "ambassador" and "affiliate" alone are likely to be judged unclear. A discount code or @mention alone is not enough. A platform paid-partnership tool may help but may not be enough alone, so add "Ad" too. Source: https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recognising-ads-social-media.html
- **[Official, TikTok]** Turn on the commercial content disclosure toggle when posting paid or gifted content. TikTok says this does not replace any legal disclosure you also owe. Source: https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/bc-policy/en
- **Practical rule [Creative advice built on the above]:** burn an "Ad" text into the first 1 to 2 seconds, say "this is an ad" or "made for [brand]" aloud, and also use the platform toggle. Check the brief for extra wording the brand wants.
- Your own legal position depends on where you and your audience are. This is not legal advice.

## Platform formats

Two kinds of source are mixed below. Some pages are written for advertisers running **paid ads** (TikTok Ads and Google Ads guidance). Their specs and best practices are recommendations for ad placements. They are not requirements for an organic post that is sponsored content. The rules that bind organic sponsored posts are the disclosure rules above and each platform's own branded content policy. Where a source below is advertiser guidance, it is marked **[Official, paid-ad guidance]**.

- **[Official, YouTube]** Square or vertical videos up to 3 minutes count as Shorts. Maximum upload resolution for Shorts is 1080p. Sources: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877 and https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070
- **[Official, paid-ad guidance, TikTok]** Ad creative is 9:16 full screen and sound-on. Stay inside the safe zone so buttons and caption do not cover key information. Source: https://www.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/tiktok-creative-ultimate-guide
- **[Official, paid-ad guidance, Google Ads]** For Shorts ads, only the first 60 seconds play in the feed; use vertical 9:16 with sound. Source: https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16041697

## Music and rights

Check the brief before adding any music. TikTok directs ad and branded content to its Commercial Music Library, since popular tracks may not be cleared for brand use (**[Official, paid-ad guidance, TikTok]** https://www.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads). Do not use footage, faces, logos or voices you do not have permission for. Do not show other people's private details on screen. Ask the brand who owns the finished video and how long they can use it; that is a contract point, not a technique point.

# 2. Safe zones (where to put text)

- **[Official, paid-ad guidance]** TikTok says to stay inside the safe zone but publishes several templates, not one number. Use the template that matches your placement from TikTok's ad specs.
- **[Third-party, measured from the platform templates]** A TikTok standard zone is about 12.5% clear at the top, 34% at the bottom, 11% each side plus a column on the right (https://terrads.com/blog/tiktok-ad-specs-safe-zone). For Reels and Stories ads, about 14% top, 35% bottom, 6% sides (https://terrads.com/blog/meta-safe-zones-are-percentages).
- **Simple working rule [Creative advice]:** on a 1080 x 1920 frame, keep text and faces between about y=250 and y=1250, and out of the right-hand strip. That fits all the figures above. Re-check against the brief's own template if it supplies one.
- Put captions in the middle-lower area of the safe region, not the very bottom.

# 3. Captions and on-screen text

- **[Official, paid-ad guidance, TikTok]** Text overlays raise view time and recall in TikTok's own studies; the app has auto captions. Source: https://www.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads
- **[Official, W3C]** Captions are a text version of speech and key sounds, synchronised with the audio. Closed captions can be turned off; open captions are burned in. Source: https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/captions/
- **[Creative advice]** Burn in captions yourself so they survive reposts. Use 3 to 6 words per line, high contrast (white with dark outline or box), one to two lines at a time. Always read the auto-caption text and fix names and numbers.

# 4. Hook and pacing

- **[Official, paid-ad guidance, TikTok own study]** TikTok reports most ad recall impact in the first 6 seconds and recommends a hook, body, call-to-action structure. It says faster scene changes tend to draw viewers in early. It is TikTok's own marketing data, from ads, not a guarantee. Source: https://www.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads
- **[Official, YouTube]** The Shorts upload screen has a "Get feedback" tool that analyses hook and pacing. Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070
- **[Creative advice]** State the point in the first line spoken. Change shot, angle or on-screen text about every 2 to 4 seconds in talking-head videos. Cut every pause and filler word. End on the call to action, not a fade.

# 5. Filming on your Samsung Galaxy

Features vary by model and One UI version. Check the menu on your phone.

- **[Official, Samsung]** Camera settings include video resolution, video stabilization, grid lines and Advanced video options (HEVC, high bitrate, HDR, Zoom-in mic). Pro Video lets you set ISO, exposure, white balance and microphone. Sources: https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10001353/ and https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10004601/
- **[Official, Samsung]** On supported models, Pro Video > MIC offers directional options (Omni, Front, Rear and others) and an input level slider. Use Front for talking to camera in a noisy place. Source: https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10003428/
- **[Official, Samsung]** Zoom-in mic raises sound as you zoom. It can make voice noisier if you zoom while talking, so check it is off when you want natural audio. Source as above.
- **[Official, Samsung]** Auto FPS brightens low light by lowering the frame rate. For fast motion or clean captions, test with it off. Source: https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10001353/
- **[Creative advice]** Shoot vertical at 1080p or higher, 30 fps. Switch HDR video off if the clip looks washed out after export or in the editor. Clean the lens. Turn on grid lines. Use the rear camera for quality, front camera when you need to see yourself.

# 6. Light and sound

These are standard filming practice rather than platform rules. **[Creative advice]**

- Light: face a window, with the light in front of you. Avoid a window behind you. At night use a lamp in front, slightly above eye level, with a second dimmer one at the side.
- Sound: record in a small furnished room (soft surfaces cut echo). Phone 20 to 30 cm from your mouth. Do a 5 second test clip and play it back with headphones before the real take.
- Level: aim for speech clearly louder than any music. Keep music low under voice. If a brief states a loudness figure, use that figure.
- Wind or noise: shoot indoors, close windows, pause fans and fridges.

# 7. Pre-post checklist

1. Brief re-read; every required line, hashtag, tag and length met.
2. Ad label burned in early and in the caption; platform toggle on.
3. Every claim true and something you have actually done or seen. No "guaranteed", no income or health claims unless the brief supplies approved wording.
4. Text inside the safe zone on both TikTok and Reels preview.
5. Captions proofread.
6. No private info, other people's faces, or unlicensed music in frame.
7. File saved as an unedited original plus the final export.

# 8. What I could not verify

- Official safe-zone pixel numbers: TikTok and Meta publish templates; the percentages above come from third-party measurements.
- Any loudness target. YouTube states a normalisation reference, but I did not reach an official page for it, so I give no figure.
- Brand-specific rules (these add to law and platform rules, never replace them). Current Bounty briefs are login-only and not read.
