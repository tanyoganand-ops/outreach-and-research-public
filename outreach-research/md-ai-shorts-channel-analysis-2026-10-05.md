# AI-tools Shorts: 5 channels analysed, 10 patterns to steal


## Read this first (honest caveats)
- Shorts-only analytics are not published anywhere. Everything below is from public counts. "Median" means the median of each channel's 20 most recent Shorts.
- Pure "ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini" Shorts from small channels get almost nothing. Samples I checked: 3 views, 12 views, 700 views. A TestMu AI "Claude Code vs Cursor" Short had 35.2K views, but that is a company channel. The big winners in the niche are not head-to-heads. They are "free / hidden / permanent fix" tool reveals and fast news reactions.
- I picked channels that actually run Shorts and sit in AI tools/models. I dropped two that looked relevant but were not: AI Search (Shorts frozen since 2024) and AI with Ryan (1.88M subs, but gadget/survival reposts, not AI tools).
- I could not get per-Short watch time, retention or swipe-away data. Hook claims are inferred from titles, lengths and the transcript text I could see.

## View distribution (what the numbers say)
- Hits are rare and huge, the rest is flat. InsiderForce: 4 of its last 20 are over 100K, the lowest is 547 views (the newest one, only hours old) and the median is 35K. Everyone else has 0 or 1 of 20 over 100K.
- Volume does not rescue weak hooks. Julian Goldie posts about 3 a day with the same "NEW X is INSANE!" template. His top Shorts are from Dec 2024 to Mar 2025 (1.26M, 695K, 460K). His recent median is 2.8K, with ones at 132 and 345 views.
- Big subscriber count does not mean big Shorts views. Matt Wolfe has 1.02M subs and a 13.5K median. InsiderForce has 63.6K subs and a 35K median.
- Sponsored/product-name titles drag the average. InsiderForce Shorts with a "- ProductName AI" suffix (Glasser AI, TopView AI MCP, Nexa Art, AgentVolt) sit at roughly 9K to 53K, while its non-sponsored "free tool/skill" Shorts reach 100K to 1.6M.

## Punch-list: 10 patterns to steal

1. Lead with "free" or "open source" plus one named tool, not "X vs Y". InsiderForce's 1.6M and 766K Shorts are both "free ... Claude Code" reveals. Its multi-model "Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok in one window" Shorts got 7K to 30K. For the Sonnet vs Gemini series, frame the result as a reveal ("The Gemini model that beat Sonnet is free to try") rather than a bare versus.
2. Ship within days of a model launch, and name the model in the title. AI Advantage's GPT-3.5 vs GPT-4 (342K) and Julian's DeepSeek R1 Short (695K) were both made while the model was fresh. A comparison is the right format only inside that window. Keep a "launch calendar" and have a 15 s cut ready on launch day.
3. One subject per Short. Most of the 15 top Shorts name a single tool or person, and only 1 is a model-vs-model comparison. If a comparison is the format, make it one winner and one reason ("Gemini wins at X"), not a 5-category scorecard.
4. Write curiosity-gap titles, 3 to 8 words, with a claim. "Midjourney Built What?!?", "He leaked Elon's secrets", "Shopping Online Is About To Change Forever". Test 2 title variants per topic.
5. Do not copy the "NEW ___ is INSANE!" template. Julian Goldie still uses it on about 3 Shorts a day and the median is now 2.8K. The template is dead on its own. It only worked with a hot topic attached.
6. Do not run sponsored product names in the title. InsiderForce's "- ProductName AI" Shorts sit well under its median. If you do sponsored work later, put it in the description.
7. Do not assume the algorithm needs under-60-second Shorts. 10 of the 15 top Shorts run 55 s or longer (InsiderForce's top three: 106, 79, 61 s; Wolfe's: 60, 104, 97 s). That is useful if the 15 s plan under-delivers: a 45 to 75 s cut with one extra proof beat is a legitimate variant. The pure model-demo Shorts at 22 to 38 s (Berman) do less well (18K to 33K), so short is not a guarantee either. Test both lengths.
8. Use a "Top 3 / Top 5 / 3 steps" list shape for how-to topics. InsiderForce's "Top 3 Claude Code Skills" (488K) and "5 MCP servers every Claude Code user needs" (374K) both did well. I did not average list vs single-tool Shorts, so treat this as a hypothesis to test.
9. Post at one fixed time every day. InsiderForce publishes at 08:03 to 08:05 Pacific (about 16:03 UK) every time I checked (6 of 6 sampled Shorts). It is a cheap, measurable habit to copy: pick one slot and keep it for 30 Shorts before judging.
10. End with a comment-for-the-asset CTA. InsiderForce's #3 Short says "Comment human and I'll send you my ebook... The system only sends it to followers." The comment gate boosts engagement signals, and it builds a lead list. For this channel the equivalent is "Comment SONNET and I'll send the full test prompts". The note in your brief about a comment CTA on the end card already fits this.
