# Free-perks v26: free stock for paid client work, with the actual limits

16 October 2026, 17:35 BST. Research addendum. No asset downloaded, account created, form submitted, software installed, third party contacted or payment made. No client material entered. Earlier versions preserved.

For an agency video, free stock is useful only if the licence covers the final delivery channel and client use. Mixkit music and sound effects have different permissions; "Free To Use" music is not free for business promotion; Pixabay music can trigger claims even with a valid licence. These distinctions matter more than the number of assets in a library.

## 1. Mixkit: three media types, different rights

Free video licence:
https://mixkit.co/license/modal/videoFree/

Restricted video licence:
https://mixkit.co/license/modal/videoRestricted/

Music licence:
https://mixkit.co/license/modal/musicFree/

Sound effects licence:
https://mixkit.co/license/modal/sfxFree/

Terms:
https://mixkit.co/terms/

### Video

Items labelled **Stock Video Free License** permit commercial and non-commercial projects. Items labelled **Stock Video Restricted License** are personal-project only. A general "free stock" search result does not settle which licence applies to an individual clip. Record the clip's own page, licence and acquisition date before production.

### Music

Mixkit's Stock Music Free License permits commercial/non-commercial projects on web or social media, including internet VOD, podcasts and adverts. It explicitly excludes CDs/DVDs, video games and TV/radio broadcast. It also prohibits music-only remixes, claiming ownership or registering the music in a rights-management service. A web advert permission is not a television advert permission.

### Sound effects

The SFX Free License expressly permits games, TV/radio broadcasts and CDs/DVDs as well as web/social/podcasts and commercial projects. The final work must incorporate the effect with other material into something larger and different. Raw redistribution, stock libraries, tool/template inclusion and source-file distribution are prohibited.

That last point is important for editable client handoff: a rendered finished video and a project folder containing reusable raw sound files are not automatically the same permitted delivery. Check the precise client workflow and asset licence before handing source files over.

### Other rights/privacy

Mixkit warns that brands and other third-party components may not be cleared. The terms grant commercial analysis/use of activity and preference data. No reason to upload client IP, confidential briefs or unique unreleased work just to browse stock. Its terms include an age condition, noted as a provider term without collecting or applying any personal status about the sender.

The browser music-licence control did not yield a visible modal in the returned page. The actual published modal endpoints were fetched successfully and used for the distinctions above. No download or claim-clearance process was tested. A permitted licence does not promise an automated platform will never issue a claim.

## 2. Pexels: free client media is not permission to imply endorsement

Licence summary:
https://www.pexels.com/license/

Full terms:
https://www.pexels.com/terms-of-service/

Current terms grant commercial/non-commercial adaptation without mandatory attribution, subject to prohibited uses. The practical restrictions are wider than "don't resell an unedited image":

- No standalone sale/distribution. A resize, crop, colour change or filter alone still counts as standalone use in the full terms.
- No implying a pictured person/brand endorses a business. An image cannot be presented as the sender's own exclusive shoot or a real client testimonial.
- No stock element as a trademark/business name/service mark.
- Full terms prohibit commercial use in relation to goods/services when the content depicts trademarks, logos or brands. That includes a recognisable brand in a client commercial, not only printing a logo on merchandise.
- Further personality, property, architecture and other rights can apply. Pexels does not warrant that releases have been obtained for every use.

The terms also say uploaded content, communications and usage/search data may be used by Pexels/partners to improve services including machine learning. An account preference can opt contributed content out of future AI training, but that is not a blanket confidentiality guarantee. We neither created an account nor uploaded anything.

For paid client work, prefer material with no recognisable people, brands or artworks unless the needed permissions are documented. That is risk reduction, not a legal clearance for an unreviewed clip.

## 3. Pixabay: valid free music can still get a platform claim

Licence summary:
https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/

Full terms, page dated 18 November 2024:
https://pixabay.com/service/terms/

Current FAQ:
https://pixabay.com/service/faq/

The current licence is royalty-free, not a claim that all current content is copyright-free. Full terms distinguish older pre 9 January 2019 published CC0 content from the newer Content License. Standalone sale/distribution, trademark use, misleading use and specific commercial uses of depicted brands are restricted. Third-party releases remain a separate check.

### Content ID

The FAQ explicitly says contributors/distributors may register music in YouTube Content ID. Automated claims can occur despite lawful use. Tracks with a shield icon are registered; a download certificate is available for those tracks, not necessarily every track in the catalogue. Keep the exact track URL, filename, acquisition evidence and the licence. A certificate is evidence for a dispute, not a promise that a client's launch will never be muted or delayed.

No music selected, downloaded or registered in a channel. No client channel linked. Do not promise "copyright-claim-free" simply because a music page says free.

### Signup friction

The FAQ says full-resolution photos/videos require signup. A free licence does not mean every download flow is account-free. This remains behind the sender's approval gate; no account created. Sponsored paid content is excluded from Pixabay's licence, so check the actual download source rather than assuming every image on the page is covered.

## 4. "Free To Use" music: monetised UGC is not client promotion

Full licence:
https://freetouse.com/license

Usage summary:
https://freetouse.com/usage-policy

The free licence covers non-premium assets in correctly attributed individual user-generated content on social platforms. Platform ad monetisation is permitted. But its definitions separate that from **commercial content**, which includes business promotion, sponsorship, paid adverts, commercial websites and any content published on behalf of a company/organisation.

Consequently, a personal vlog earning platform ad revenue may fit while an unpaid agency portfolio advert or a client reel does not. "We aren't charging for this video" is not enough. The paid Personal Plan also excludes commercial content; its name does not solve a client-use gap.

Commercial/digital-product/broadcast use requires the relevant paid licence. No price quoted here because no plan selected and taxes/territory/final checkout were not verified. No paid plan, single-track licence, channel whitelist or signup started.

The licence forbids giving away raw tracks, claiming authorship, defeating fingerprinting and AI training. Do not change pitch/speed simply to evade a Content ID claim. This is a licence restriction, not an instruction to perform a workaround.

## 5. Resolve Free: useful editor, not every Studio feature for £0

Official comparison:
https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve/studio/

Blackmagic's page says free Resolve can edit and finish up to 60 fps at Ultra HD 3840 x 2160, with grading/editing/audio tools. Studio adds features such as Neural Engine/extra FX, advanced noise reduction and expanded hardware/format options. The benefit is the free editor, not a cash credit or an entitlement to every Studio AI feature.

No installer/EULA, hardware compatibility or download-registration field tested. A chosen machine, footage codec and needed effect should be checked before switching an active paid client project to it. No paid Studio purchase proposed.

## A safe acquisition checklist when the sender chooses a real project

1. Identify the exact clip/track and exact end use: paid client, web/social, TV/radio, game, editable template or source-file handoff.
2. Save the asset page, the licence applying at acquisition and any certificate/release. This report is not a substitute for that evidence.
3. Confirm any model/property/brand permissions. Avoid claiming endorsement or exclusive authorship.
4. Check whether signup or payment is required and ask the sender before either. The sender submits applications; this workflow has submitted none.
5. Test the final platform privately using an authorised account only after the sender chooses that workflow. No such test happened here.

No account or prior acquisition history was reconciled. These are researched options, not confirmed unused entitlements or outstanding tasks.
