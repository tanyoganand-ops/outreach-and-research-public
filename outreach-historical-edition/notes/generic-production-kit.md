# Generic production brief and filming templates

Historical source date: 23 October 2026. Re-edited 16 November 2026. Selected generic material from three historical production-pack versions, with no version designated latest. This is not a brand assignment, a tested workflow on a reader's device, an asset pack or a permission to post. Times are planning estimates, not measured production guarantees. Policy/interface claims require a fresh check for actual use.

## Order of decisions

Law, platform requirements, disclosure, consent and rights come before a brief. A brief cannot licence someone else's music, footage, likeness or original expression unless the necessary rights actually exist. Do not assume an in-app asset or publicly viewable reference is cleared for commercial reuse.

## Format 1: talking head

Keep an Ad label clear from the first beat through the end in these templates, separate from the closing CTA; use required platform tools too. Actual legal disclosure depends on the context and country.

- 0-2 seconds: one truthful problem/result hook. First-person experience only if real.
- 2-8 seconds: the genuine situation, stated simply.
- 8-20 seconds: the product doing two or three actual tasks, one per beat.
- 20-27 seconds: a specific result or limitation actually observed.
- 27-30 seconds: an accurate agreed call to action.

Shoot a main take plus useful close/detail shots. Captions should be high contrast and checked in the real placement preview, away from controls. These timings are creative examples, not platform limits.

## Format 2: skit / point of view

- Setup: [recognisable situation].
- Conflict: [a clear problem without fabricated testimony].
- Turn: [show one real product function].
- Payoff: [an illustrative reaction, not a false outcome claim].
- End card: [truthful CTA].

One location and one prop can keep the example simple. Use on-screen setup text so it works muted. Keep the Ad label visible in the content; fictional acting must not be presented as a real customer endorsement.

## Format 3: original work informed by a reference

Analyse a lawfully accessible reference, but create original wording, setting, structure and expression. Cosmetic changes do not establish rights. Copying timing, sound, distinctive creative elements or footage may require permission. Do not download/repost the reference or assume a brief's reference link supplies a licence. Check rights for the exact audio, commercial use and platform. If unclear, choose original/cleared material or voice-only.

## Format 4: product or screen demonstration

Use a supported task, actual input, real steps, actual output and one honest limitation. A number belongs in the hook only if measured. Hide notifications, names, contacts, balances and private information before recording; do not rely only on a late crop. Use a demo document rather than another person's contract or account. Add the Ad label and required platform disclosure separately from the CTA.

## Format 5: text-overlay edit

The source suggested 6-10 short clips, one idea per clip, about 1.5-2.5 seconds each. That is a creative example, not a required length. Opening text states the point; final text is an accurate CTA. Keep captions readable and Ad disclosure visible. An asset being available in an editor does not establish the needed music or footage licence.

## Example production workflow

Read the real brief and unresolved questions, write a short script, set up vertical framing/light/audio, shoot multiple takes, record supporting detail, edit, add captions, review and export. The source estimated 60-90 minutes per short video. That estimate is not a delivery commitment and excludes unresolved permissions, product testing, approvals and revisions.

For supported phone/editor combinations, the source suggested vertical 9:16, 1080 p, 30 fps as a working starting point. Device features vary. Check actual exports rather than assuming every editor/platform accepts the same codec, frame rate or colour profile.

## Hooks, only when truthful

- "Here is what [product] actually does in [measured duration]."
- "I tried [specific task] and noticed [real result/limitation]."
- "Three things I wish I knew before [real experience]."
- "This took me [measured time], compared with [measured baseline]."
- "One setting that matters for [specific task]."

Do not use an untested comparison, invented result or fictional customer as proof. A concept label does not make a misleading performance claim acceptable.

## Pre-publication checklist

1. Correct real brief, length and placement; no outstanding rights/claim question hidden.
2. Clear Ad label in the content, required platform tool and suitable caption disclosure. A buried hashtag or closing CTA alone is not assumed sufficient.
3. Every statement true and substantiated. No invented experience, income, conversion, health or legal-accuracy claim.
4. Actual safe-zone preview checked. The source's numerical margin estimates are not universal platform rules.
5. Captions proofread; audio intelligible; entire export watched with sound and muted.
6. No unauthorised people, private information, music, logos, screens or footage.
7. Correct usage rights, consent, fee, review and posting permission. A template is not that approval.
8. Original project/export saved with version/date. Publication status is recorded accurately, not assumed.
