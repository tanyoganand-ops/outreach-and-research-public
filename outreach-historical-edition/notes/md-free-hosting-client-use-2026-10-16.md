## The decision-changing distinction

**Vercel Hobby is limited to non-commercial personal use. Netlify says its free tier permits commercial projects.** That does not make Netlify operationally unlimited or justify a migration without checking the specific site requirements.

### Vercel: client work is commercial even without a checkout

Official fair-use guidelines recorded in the source:
https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines

Hobby documentation recorded in the source:
https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby

The published definition includes any deployment used for financial gain by anyone involved in producing the project, including a paid developer/consultant. Examples include advertising products/services and receiving payment to create, update or host a site. A site does not need to take payments itself to be commercial.

Hobby usage caps and the paid Pro/Enterprise permission are separate questions. Staying under the free bandwidth quota does not remove a commercial-use restriction. Conversely, a personal experiment can be legitimate on Hobby without becoming a paid subscription merely because it uses Vercel.

### Netlify: commercial permission, but a hard-stop allowance

Netlify's own comparison guide:
https://www.netlify.com/guides/netlify-vs-vercel/

Credit-based plan documentation recorded in the source:
https://docs.netlify.com/manage/accounts-and-billing/billing/billing-for-credit-based-plans/credit-based-pricing-plans


Free has 300 credits per month, a credit hard limit and no auto-recharge. Credits are usage units, not $300. The historical source also records a provider all-project pause on a team when free credit is exhausted; separate add-ons/domains can still have costs. A hard stop protects from usage overage but can take a client site offline. It is not a reliability guarantee.

## Scope

Permission for commercial use is separate from resource limits, reliability, add-on charges, security and suitability for a particular site. A site's plan, workload and requirements need checking before deployment or migration. Historical free-tier figures are not unused entitlements or a statement that any specific site complies.
