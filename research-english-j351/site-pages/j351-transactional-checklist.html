Writing checklist: AO5 + AO6

Use this before and after a practice answer. It is a review tool, not an extra mark scheme or a list of compulsory devices.

40 marks: AO5, communication and organisation, 24. AO6, vocabulary, sentence structures, spelling and punctuation, 16. Review both separately.

Before you write

  • Have I chosen one of the two tasks and understood its whole brief?
  • Who am I writing or speaking to? What do they already know, care about or have power to change?
  • What form is required: letter, article or speech/talk? What purpose or combination of purposes must I fulfil?
  • Which relevant ideas can I develop with explanation, detail, examples or reasoning?
  • Does the planned order help the reader follow my ideas? Have I allowed myself time to check the ending?

AO5: communication and organisation

  • Does the response answer the task rather than a memorised topic?
  • Are my tone, style and register suitable for this audience and sustained through the piece?
  • Is the form recognisable without wasting time on irrelevant layout?
  • Do important ideas develop beyond assertions? Is the support relevant and credible?
  • Do paragraphs group ideas sensibly and connect to the whole piece?
  • Does the ending fulfil the purpose, whether that means informing, advising, arguing, persuading or combining purposes?

AO6: control and accuracy

  • Are sentence boundaries clear, with no accidental run-ons or comma splices?
  • Are different sentence structures used securely and for a reason?
  • Does punctuation clarify meaning? Have I removed punctuation I cannot control?
  • Is vocabulary precise and suitable rather than complicated for its own sake?
  • Have I checked agreement, apostrophes, homophones, common spelling slips and omitted words?
  • Is the final paragraph as controlled and readable as the opening?

Do not turn advice into invented rules

  • No compulsory semicolon, fragment, tricolon, rhetorical question or concession.
  • No fixed paragraph count, equal paragraph length, exact sentence quota or mandatory single-argument formula.
  • No address needed for the letter task; an appropriate greeting and sign-off are enough. Never use your personal address.
  • No automatic whole-level form penalty. Form, audience and purpose remain part of the AO5 judgement.
  • No reliable numerical grade can be inferred from one attractive short extract. A qualification grade is not the same thing as a writing-task mark.

After review: choose the next useful change

Write one specific AO5 improvement and one AO6 improvement, then revise a paragraph. For example: "explain why the headteacher should care about this practical problem" and "replace comma splices with controlled sentence boundaries". These are examples of useful targets, not compulsory targets for every answer.
